VPN and Tailscale

A recap.

VPNs

VPN (Virtual Private Network)
A protected network connection over existing infrastructure, usually the internet. “Virtual” means no dedicated physical connection; “private” means traffic is protected with encryption. It does not inherently mean hiding your IP or changing your location.
Encrypted tunnel
Carries traffic securely between endpoints. Observers along the route cannot read its encrypted contents; the endpoints can.
Work VPN vs. consumer VPN
A work VPN connects your device to company resources. A consumer VPN routes browsing through a provider’s server; websites see that server’s public IP instead of yours.

Tailscale

Tailscale
A mesh VPN connecting authorized devices — laptops, phones, servers — into a private network called a tailnet. Uses WireGuard for encryption.
Mesh
Devices connect directly where possible, rather than always through one central VPN server. Tailscale’s coordination service helps establish connections; relays carry encrypted traffic when direct connections fail, without decrypting it.
Access controls
Define which users and devices can reach which resources.
Exit node
A device selected to route internet traffic. Without one, Tailscale generally leaves ordinary browsing unchanged; with one, browsing exits through that device’s internet connection.
Scope
Tailscale supplies connectivity, not file-sharing or remote-desktop functionality. It runs over your existing internet and generally avoids manual router port forwarding or exposing private services publicly.
